Act 0 — Access

Every session starts with a tenant

FORGE is multi-tenant to the query layer, not to a config flag. Who you sign in as decides which product you get: a forwarder sees an operations console, a shipper sees only its own cargo, and a partner agent sees its own book of business plus what it owes the platform. Pick a seat and the rest of this walkthrough follows that person.

A customer's cargo belongs to the forwarder's tenant — the forwarder booked it. So the portal cannot use the tenant predicate; it scopes by the parties a forwarder has explicitly linked. Under the hood — how this screen is built

The map does not summarise the rows below it. Counting a page of shipments would have described the first fifty and called it the book — so lanes come from GET /ops/corridors, a database-side aggregate over the whole book with open-exception counts per lane. The list underneath is keyset-paginated on (created_at, id); offsets skip and repeat rows when freight is booked while an operator pages.

SQL/ops/corridorscount(distinct shipment) group by (origin, destination), left join open exceptions
SQL/shipmentswhere tenant_id = $caller order by created_at desc, id desc limit 51
Act 2 — Price

Buy rates, and the margin on top

Two tables decide every number this business earns. Rate cards are what the carrier charges; margin rules are what you charge on top. Quoting reads straight from here — which means pricing a new corridor is a commercial decision, not an engineering ticket.

What the freight costs you

Rate cards

What you charge on top

Margin rules

Most specific wins. Customer beats lane beats mode; the all-null rule is the tenant default. Ties break by recency — two rules of equal scope used to resolve to whichever row Postgres returned first, so the same quote could price at 15% or 18% on two runs with nothing changed.
Under the hood — surcharges are a set, not a diff

A tariff arrives as a sheet, so surcharges are replaced wholesale rather than patched row by row: "these are the costs now" is the only statement that cannot leave a withdrawn bunker adjustment behind. PERCENT_OF_FREIGHT reuses the amount column for basis points, so it is capped at 10 000 — a 15% BAF typed as 150000 would otherwise bill 1 500% of freight.

Under the hood — an unpriced lane is a 422, not a 500

"We don't price that corridor" is an ordinary answer to an ordinary request, so NoRateError and NoMarginRuleError map to 422 carrying the lane in the message. Found live: quoting an unpriced lane used to log a stack trace and return 500 Internal server error, which tells a customer nothing and buries a real configuration gap under noise.

Act 7 — The invoice

One standard document, whoever is billing

Charges are written by the event consumers as the freight moves — nobody keys them in. What comes out is not a total and a due date: it is a consolidated multi-party billing document, with every line stating whose cost it carries and whether it was passed through at cost, marked up, or originated by the forwarder. No forwarder invoice in the market carries that last field.

Under the hood — what makes this multi-company rather than one forwarder's system

Every company on the platform issues under its own registration, its own VAT and customs client numbers, its own bank account and its own gapless numbering series — incremented inside the issuing transaction rather than drawn from a shared sequence, because several jurisdictions require an issuer's numbering to be sequential with no gaps, and a shared sequence hands company B the numbers company A did not use. The issuer and bill-to blocks are snapshotted onto the invoice: a company that changes its bank account tomorrow must not change what today's invoice says.

Act 8 — Assurance

Four-way matching, before the money leaves

Roughly four freight invoices in five carry a discrepancy, and overcharges average eight to ten per cent of the affected line. Most accounts-payable teams run a two-way match — invoice against quote total — which catches almost none of it, because the errors live inside lines nobody quoted.         Under the hood — why the findings are persisted rather than computed on read
        

          

            A finding is a thing a person acts on — accepts, queries, resolves — so its state has to
            outlive the request. Re-running the match upserts on a unique index, so opening the
            screen twice does not double the list, and a human's decision is never reset by a
            re-run. Querying a line marks the charge, and the document holds that amount back
            from the balance while still showing it: netting it off would quietly reissue the
            invoice.

RULEVAT_ON_DISBURSEMENToutput tax on a recovered statutory amount overstates the invoice and the VAT return together

          
RULESERVICE_NOT_EVIDENCEDa delivery charge on a container that never left the terminal is work not done

        Under the hood — the one deliberate cross-tenant read
        

          

            A customer's cargo belongs to the forwarder's tenant, so the portal cannot use
            tenant_id = caller. It resolves
            parties.customer_tenant_id to a set of party ids and filters
            bookings by it. No link, no visibility — invisible is the default. An unknown shipment
            returns null rather than 403, so probing cannot distinguish
            "not yours" from "doesn't exist".
